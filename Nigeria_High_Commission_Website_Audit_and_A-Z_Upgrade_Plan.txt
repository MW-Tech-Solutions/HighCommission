NIGERIA HIGH COMMISSION, NAIROBI, KENYA
WEBSITE COMPARISON AND A-Z MODERNISATION PLAN
Prepared: 4 October 2026
Purpose: proposal, design brief, implementation scope and delivery checklist.

1. RECOMMENDED DIRECTION
========================
Build a distinctive diplomatic website with a practical consular service portal.
The proposal should demonstrate three things together: a premium public website,
clear journeys for citizens and visitors, and an efficient workspace for staff.

The opportunity is to help people complete the right task with confidence:
find a service -> understand requirements -> take the correct next step ->
receive confirmation -> follow progress where the Mission controls the process.

Use elegant Nigerian green, warm white, restrained gold accents, strong typography,
authentic photography and generous spacing. Put services before lengthy welcomes.
Provide a fast, accessible experience on mobile, with complete desktop layouts.
Make approved content and reliable operations the foundation of the design.

2. REVIEW METHOD AND LIMITS
==========================
Site A: https://nigeriankenya.or.ke/
Site B: https://nigeriahighcommissionkenya.org/

The client identifies A as the official site and B as an imitation/proposal.
This report calls B the comparison site. Domain ownership, authorisation and
copying were not independently established by this review.

Reviewed public homepages through live Chrome rendering, downloaded HTML and
desktop/mobile screenshots. Viewports: 1440 x 1000 and 390 x 844 CSS pixels.
Reviewed A's passport, visa requirements, citizen registration and enquiries
pages as public page content. Reviewed B's consular index, visa and contact
pages in the browser. Findings apply to these sampled pages on the review date.

Both homepages returned HTTP 200 in the browser and had no horizontal document
overflow at the two sampled widths. This is a limited layout check, not full
responsive, accessibility, browser-compatibility or performance certification.
Both mobile menu controls opened navigation in the sampled browser. On B,
selecting Tourist Visa changed the displayed requirements, and Ask the Mission
opened an assistance panel with suggested questions. No chat query was sent.
Full-page screenshots can catch lazy-loaded images before they appear; blank
image areas are not treated here as confirmed missing assets.

No enquiries, applications, payments or registrations were submitted. No private
admin systems were accessed. A visible button or form does not establish that
its backend works. No security penetration testing or real-user speed study was
performed. Current fees, eligibility, processing times, personnel, payment routes
and immigration policies need Mission approval before reuse.

Evidence: website-audit-evidence/ contains screenshots and extracted public page
details. Source references are listed at the end of this document.

3. EXISTING DESIGN FLOWS
=======================
SITE A: OFFICIAL SITE AS IDENTIFIED BY THE CLIENT
Sources: [1]-[5]; local official desktop/mobile screenshots.

Homepage sequence:
Date/search/contact strip -> logo and dropdown navigation -> large photographic
slideshow -> notices and welcome -> second introduction -> tourism/trade links
-> newsletter strip -> institutional links and location footer.

Visual character: green and pale green backgrounds, conventional navigation,
large event photography, mixed typography and white content boxes.
The mobile layout stacks sections beneath a compact menu.

Strengths: recognisable identity, substantial service information, public notices,
government links and an existing citizen-registration route.

Improvement opportunities: the hero occupies much of the first desktop screen;
service actions lack a prominent task panel; introductions repeat; mobile users
must scroll through substantial content. The public warning is useful but could
be a concise, consistently styled alert. Source code indicates WordPress assets;
no conclusions about its maintenance or security follow from that alone.

Sample service journeys:
- Passport: menu -> instructions -> external immigration application/appointment
  systems. The renewal section includes a lost-passport letter instruction;
  separate these scenarios and have an officer verify the text. [2]
- Visa: menu -> long requirements page -> external application. Divide approved
  categories into consistent, scannable service pages. [3]
- Registration: explanation -> downloadable form or separate citizen subdomain.
  Existing online registration must be assessed before choosing migration or
  integration; it is not a completely absent capability. [4]
- Enquiries: physical address, contact channels and a form are published. Improve
  service routing and acknowledgement; delivery was not tested. [5]

SITE B: COMPARISON SITE
Sources: [6]-[9]; local comparison screenshots and extracted page details.

Homepage sequence:
Floating rounded navigation -> large six-slide photographic hero and two actions
-> Mission introduction and national statistics -> image-led service cards ->
Nigeria map/country information -> quick-help cards -> notices -> tourism feature
-> citizen-registration banner -> multi-column footer.

Visual character: rounded panels, strong display headings, green actions,
cream/light content areas, dark green footer and photography with overlays.
There is a floating assistance button and a mobile bottom navigation bar.
HTML assets identify a Next.js frontend; the backend architecture is unknown.

Strengths: stronger visual hierarchy than A, clearer service cards, obvious visa
action, deliberate mobile navigation and a more consistent component style.
The consular landing page groups services; the visa page exposes category
controls and short summary panels. An enquiry form is visible on the contact
page. These are useful foundations to learn from without copying the design.

Improvement opportunities:
- The near-full-screen hero and introduction delay the main service catalogue.
- Many homepage sections produce a long mobile journey.
- Small navigation and image-overlay copy deserve careful readability testing.
- The visible contact page gives Nairobi, Kenya without a full street address.
- Public statistics need dates, definitions and authoritative sources.
- An assistance control is present, but its existence does not prove a staffed
  helpdesk, authoritative answer system or useful case-management capability.
- The sampled pages emphasise guidance and links. A secure applicant dashboard,
  working booking engine and staff workflow were not demonstrated in this review.

Visa journey: prominent visa action -> category requirements -> external portal
instructions. Citizen registration is also promoted on the homepage.
The strongest differentiation is an improved, demonstrable service journey.

6. PROPOSED HOMEPAGE FLOW AND VISUAL DIRECTION
============================================
01. Slim identity strip: approved official-site wording and urgent notice if active.
02. Clear header: coat of arms, full Mission name, navigation, search and portal.
03. Compact hero: one strong authorised image with a concise public-service message.
04. Beside/within hero: Find a service; Passport; Visa; Citizen Registration;
    Emergency Help. Each action describes what happens next.
05. Popular services: six simple cards with icon, description and precise action.
06. Current service updates: dated notices and relevant closure information.
07. How to access services: prepare -> apply/book through the right system -> follow up.
08. Nigerians in Kenya: registration, welfare and community links.
09. Mission introduction: short statement, approved portrait and link to full details.
10. News/events: a small selection with dates and an accessible archive.
11. Trade and tourism: two distinct editorial features.
12. Visit/contact panel: location, service-specific hours and directions.
13. Footer: verified contacts, official links, policies, accessibility and feedback.

Desktop concept:
  [Identity / active alert]
  [Crest + Mission name] [Navigation] [Search] [My Portal]
  [Headline + authorised photograph] [What do you need? Task panel]
  [Passport] [Visa] [Registration] [Documents] [Enquiries] [Emergency]
  [Service updates] [How it works]
  [Community] [Mission] [News] [Trade/Tourism] [Visit us] [Footer]

Mobile concept:
  [Identity + menu] -> [Concise headline] -> [Task panel] -> [Active notice]
  -> [Services] -> [Remaining editorial sections] -> [Contact + footer].
  Optional bottom actions: Services, My requests, Help. Validate their usefulness
  in testing; they must not cover form fields, errors or cookie choices.

Suggested starting design tokens, subject to contrast testing:
- Primary green #006B45; deep green #063D2D; warm white #F7F8F4.
- Main text #172A23; restrained accent gold #B68A35; neutral borders #DCE5DF.
- Gold is decorative unless a tested text/background pairing passes contrast.
- One highly readable body font; at most one complementary heading family.
- Body text generally 16-18px; desktop headings 40-56px; mobile headings 28-36px.
- Comfortable line lengths, consistent spacing, restrained corners and shadows.
- Use meaningful photos of diplomacy, public service and Nigeria/Kenya relations.
- Avoid automatic background video and unnecessary animation; honour reduced motion.
- Confirm rights to photographs, seals and portraits; use approved current assets.

7. A-Z IMPLEMENTATION WORK PLAN
===============================
Priority key:
P0 = required public launch foundation.
P1 = first operational portal release after workflow/data approval.
P2 = optional enhancement after the core service is established.

A. AUDIT AND AGREED SCOPE [P0]
Inventory every existing URL, document, form, subdomain and external dependency.
Interview consular staff and representative visitors. Establish top tasks,
service volumes, common mistakes and ownership of each workflow.
Deliver: signed requirements, content inventory, risk/dependency log and backlog.
Done when: every existing important service has a retain/rewrite/merge/archive
decision and a named owner; launch scope and exclusions are explicit.

B. BRAND AND DESIGN SYSTEM [P0]
Create an original visual identity using approved national/Mission assets.
Design typography, colours, spacing, layout grids, icons, buttons, forms, cards,
alerts, navigation, tables, empty states, loading states and error messages.
Deliver: reusable component library with mobile and desktop examples.
Done when: approved templates use the same components and tested colour pairs.

C. CONTENT REWRITE AND VALIDATION [P0]
Rewrite instructions in plain English and remove repetition. Assign a content
owner, source, approval status, last-review date and next-review date.
Verify all service facts with the Mission; resolve contradictory instructions.
Deliver: approved content pack, media library and reusable service template.
Done when: every published service has a complete, current, approved record.

D. DIGITAL SERVICE DIRECTORY [P0]
Provide browse and search routes based on the visitor's task. A short finder
may ask about the service and situation without collecting identity documents.
Deliver: service directory, category pages and rule-based guided signposting.
Done when: each top task has a clear route to requirements and the next action,
including exceptions and the correct authority responsible for the service.

E. EMERGENCY ASSISTANCE [P0]
Give urgent assistance a prominent, persistent route. Clearly distinguish
emergency support from routine passport/visa enquiries and published hours.
Deliver: approved emergency page, verified click-to-call actions and escalation
instructions; no invented 24-hour availability or unstaffed promises.
Done when: users can find the appropriate emergency contact without signing in.

F. FORMS AND DOCUMENT HANDLING [P1]
Use short, labelled steps, inline validation, an error summary and a review page.
Support draft saving, safe retry, clear submission receipts and accessible help.
Only collect documents that an approved workflow requires. Use private storage,
file type/size checks, malware scanning, limited access and retention rules.
Done when: interrupted sessions recover safely and resubmission cannot silently
create duplicate cases; rejected uploads give useful, accessible guidance.

G. GOVERNANCE AND OWNERSHIP [P0]
Define product owner, content approvers, technical owner and service owners.
Record the Mission's ownership/access for domain, hosting, code, backups, design
assets, email and paid service accounts. Establish change and publishing rules.
Deliver: responsibility matrix, approval workflow and account/access register.
Done when: the Mission can operate and transfer the service without depending
on one contractor's personal accounts.

H. HOMEPAGE AND NAVIGATION [P0]
Build the proposed service-led homepage and audience-based navigation.
Give desktop dropdowns and mobile menus clear labels, focus handling and close
controls. Keep the complete site name readable and the main actions visible.
Deliver: homepage, header, footer, search entry and breadcrumb components.
Done when: test users locate the five priority tasks without staff assistance.

I. INTEGRATIONS AND AUTHORITY BOUNDARIES [P0/P1]
Identify which services belong to the Mission and which belong to Nigeria
Immigration Service or another authority. Verify every external destination.
Launch with clearly labelled official handoff links when no approved API exists.
Add API integrations only after access, contracts, testing and ownership are clear.
Done when: users understand where they are applying and no local dashboard
claims live national passport/visa status without an authorised data source.

J. JOURNEY PROTOTYPES [P0]
Prototype passport guidance, visa guidance, citizen registration, routine
enquiry and emergency help. Show both the happy path and common failures.
Use synthetic demonstration data, clearly labelled as such.
Deliver: clickable mobile/desktop prototype and a short stakeholder demo script.
Done when: feedback produces a revised flow before expensive implementation.

K. KNOWLEDGE BASE AND SEARCH [P0; ASSISTANT P2]
Create approved FAQs and useful search across public services, news and downloads.
Support common task phrases and clear no-results suggestions. Exclude private
records, drafts, expired guidance and citizen identifiers from the search index.
An optional assistant must cite approved pages, show review dates and route
uncertainty to staff. It must not invent eligibility decisions or collect IDs.
Done when: the agreed top enquiry phrases return the correct current guidance.

L. LANGUAGE AND ACCESSIBILITY [P0; EXTRA LANGUAGES P2]
Target WCAG 2.2 AA using automated checks plus manual keyboard and screen-reader
testing. Include semantic headings, visible focus, clear labels, useful errors,
text alternatives, zoom/reflow and reduced-motion behaviour. [10]
Start with approved English; add professionally reviewed Kiswahili if justified
by audience research and supported by an ongoing translation owner.
Done when: representative complete journeys pass the agreed accessibility review.

M. MOBILE AND RESPONSIVE BEHAVIOUR [P0]
Design the smallest layouts first and expand to tablet and desktop. Test 320,
360, 390, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920px widths, orientation changes and text zoom.
Aim for touch controls at least 44px high as a project usability target.
Use correct input types, autofill and one-column mobile forms. Keep essential
guidance readable even if decorative images fail.
Done when: no essential content is clipped, hidden or covered by fixed controls.

N. NEWS, NOTICES AND EVENTS [P0]
Separate urgent alerts, service notices, diplomatic news and events. Give each
dates, category, owner and an appropriate expiry/archive rule.
Allow scheduled publishing and preview. Surface relevant notices on service
pages, not only on the homepage. Provide accessible galleries and document links.
Done when: an editor can publish and retire a notice without developer support.

O. ONLINE APPOINTMENTS [P1, MISSION-MANAGED SERVICES ONLY]
First confirm which appointments are already managed by external authorities.
For approved local bookings, define service capacity, durations, holidays,
lead time, cutoffs, rescheduling, cancellation, reminders and staff overrides.
Store times consistently and show the Africa/Nairobi time zone to applicants.
Done when: concurrent requests cannot reserve the same last slot; closures and
cancellations behave correctly. Preserve external booking routes where required.

P. PORTAL ACCOUNTS AND REQUEST TRACKING [P1]
Provide verified accounts, secure recovery, clear session management and a
dashboard for Mission-managed requests, drafts, appointments and messages.
Offer a secure reference-based follow-up route where full accounts add friction.
Use statuses such as Received, Under review, More information needed and Closed,
with service-specific states approved by staff.
Done when: each person sees only their own authorised records and understands
what the displayed status covers. A local receipt is not a visa/passport approval.

Q. QUALITY ASSURANCE AND ACCEPTANCE [P0/P1]
Test critical journeys, role permissions, form errors, file handling, session
expiry, duplicate submissions, unavailable integrations and notification failures.
Run accessibility, device/browser, performance and authorised security reviews.
Agree a defect severity model and who accepts the release.
Done when: no unresolved critical defects remain and owners sign off their flows.

R. REGISTRATION AND COMMUNITY SERVICES [P1]
Assess the existing citizen-registration system before rebuilding it. Agree
minimum fields, verification, duplicate handling, updates and records migration.
Separate service registration from optional community/newsletter subscriptions.
Keep the citizen registry private; any public association/business directory
requires an explicit approved publication process.
Done when: citizens can register/update details and staff can review them safely.

S. SECURITY AND DATA PROTECTION [P0/P1]
Require staff MFA, least-privilege roles, secure sessions, server-side validation,
encryption, secret management, audit records, patching and abuse controls.
Prevent unauthorised record access and keep uploaded documents out of public URLs.
Agree lawful processing, retention, hosting location, vendor access and incident
procedures with the Mission's responsible privacy/legal team.
Use OWASP ASVS to define a relevant verification checklist. [13]
Done when: approved controls are implemented and independently tested to scope.

T. TRADE, TOURISM AND BILATERAL RELATIONS [P0]
Create attractive, curated editorial pages with approved photography and useful
sector/travel resources. Date and source economic figures and policy claims.
Provide a structured trade-enquiry route with a named receiving team.
Deliver: reusable feature, destination, sector and bilateral-news templates.
Done when: content supports a real visitor action and can be updated by editors.

U. USER SUPPORT AND STAFF WORKSPACE [P1]
Route enquiries by service. Give each request an acknowledgement and reference,
assign a responsible officer and record replies, internal notes and changes.
Add overdue queues, search, filters, saved responses and controlled exports.
Publish response expectations only when staff capacity supports them.
Done when: a request can move from intake through response to closure with a
complete history, while internal notes remain invisible to applicants.

V. VERIFIED IDENTITY AND COMMUNICATIONS [P0]
Use a Mission-approved canonical domain and verified contact directory. Keep
public website and email identity consistent. Publish an approved official-site
verification page and service-charge guidance; avoid unsupported accusations.
Configure domain/email protection, trusted sender identities and alerts for
certificate/domain expiry. Establish a route for reporting suspicious messages.
Done when: official channels and permitted payment destinations are unambiguous.

W. WEB PERFORMANCE AND RELIABILITY [P0/P1]
Optimise images, prioritise the hero asset, defer nonessential embeds and avoid
loading all slideshow images eagerly. Cache public pages without caching private
portal responses. Add uptime/error monitoring, backups and restore exercises.
Aim for field p75 LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms and CLS <=0.1. [11]
Done when: agreed lab/device budgets pass before launch; field targets are
monitored after sufficient real-user data becomes available.

X. EXPORTS, MIGRATION AND SEARCH DISCOVERY [P0/P1]
Map old URLs to new destinations with redirects. Preserve useful titles and
downloads; remove outdated documents from normal navigation and clearly archive.
Add sitemap, canonical URLs, metadata and appropriate structured public data.
Migrate private records only through a separately approved, reconciled process.
Done when: important old links work, migrated counts reconcile, private pages
are excluded from indexing and the rollback path has been rehearsed.

Y. YEAR-ROUND OPERATIONS AND MEASUREMENT [P0/P1]
Measure task discovery, successful submissions, enquiry volume, response times,
appointment no-shows and search failures without exposing personal data.
Train editors and officers; document publishing, access removal, incident response,
backup restoration and supplier renewal responsibilities.
Done when: the Mission has trained owners, runbooks and a recurring review calendar.

Z. ZERO-CRITICAL-DEFECT LAUNCH AND IMPROVEMENT [P0/P1]
Use a staging environment, final content freeze, verified backups, launch checklist
and monitored release window. Confirm rollback triggers and decision authority.
Provide a supported launch period and a prioritised improvement backlog.
Done when: public journeys, staff operations, notifications and monitoring work
in production and the Mission accepts the handover.
This is a critical-defect release gate, not a promise of zero possible defects.

8. CORE USER FLOWS TO DEMONSTRATE
================================
Passport guidance:
Home -> Passport -> Select scenario -> Approved checklist and any prerequisite
clearance -> Verified immigration application/appointment destination -> Clearly
explained follow-up route. Never force an unnecessary local application first.

Visa guidance:
Home -> Visa -> Travel-purpose/category guidance -> Approved requirements,
payment route and processing information -> External official application.
Add local steps only if the Mission confirms that they are required.

Citizen registration:
Home -> Register -> Purpose/privacy explanation -> Verify contact -> Complete
minimal form -> Review -> Submit -> Receipt -> Officer review -> Update profile.
Include duplicate-registration and expired-verification recovery paths.

Routine enquiry:
Service page -> Related FAQ -> Contact service team -> Short form -> Reference
and acknowledgement -> Assigned officer -> Secure reply -> Closure/feedback.

Emergency:
Any page -> Emergency Help -> Urgent situation guidance -> Verified contact.
Do not require account creation or a chatbot conversation to reach urgent help.

Staff case handling:
MFA sign-in -> Assigned queue -> Read request -> Request information or act ->
Record outcome -> Notify applicant -> Close -> Audit and reporting.

9. SERVICE PAGE TEMPLATE
========================
Every service page should answer:
1. What is this service, and who is responsible for it?
2. Who can use it, and which scenario applies?
3. What documents or information are needed?
4. Which steps happen online, externally or in person?
5. What approved charges apply, in which currency, and where are they paid?
6. What published processing estimate applies, if one is authorised?
7. Is an appointment required, and who manages it?
8. How can the visitor follow up or get help?
9. When was the guidance reviewed, and by which content owner?

Display a precise primary action such as View requirements, Continue to the
official application portal, Book a Mission appointment or Send an enquiry.
Do not label an information-only page as a completed application.

10. IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH
===========================
Provisional architecture: one maintainable application with clear public,
citizen and staff areas, a structured content system, relational database,
private document storage and background notification processing.

If the delivery team supports PHP, Laravel is a reasonable candidate for the
portal, with server-rendered public pages and targeted interactive components.
Its documented facilities include authentication, authorisation, validation,
queues, mail and storage. [12] Select a supported release and compatible runtime
after checking production requirements; the presence of an XAMPP workspace does
not establish that its installed PHP is suitable for the eventual deployment.

The design is independent of framework choice. Decide technology during discovery
using team expertise, hosting rules, existing registration systems, maintenance
budget and available integrations. A frontend framework alone does not deliver
citizen services or staff workflows.

Core records: Service, Requirement, Notice, Article, Event, MediaAsset, User,
CitizenProfile, Request, RequestStatusHistory, Message, Appointment,
AppointmentSlot, Document, Notification, ContentRevision, Role and AuditEvent.
Add a payment record only if local payment collection is authorised.

Access boundaries:
- Visitor: public information and permitted enquiry submission.
- Citizen: own profile, drafts, requests and permitted documents.
- Officer: assigned/authorised service records and approved actions.
- Editor: public content without routine access to citizen documents.
- Publisher: content approval and release.
- Administrator: account/configuration functions with audited privileged access.
- Auditor: appropriately scoped read access to audit records.

Environments: isolated development, staging with synthetic data, and production.
Mission-controlled repositories and accounts; documented releases and rollback.
No production personal data in prototype screenshots, analytics or error reports.

11. DELIVERY PHASES AND INDICATIVE SCHEDULE
==========================================
Planning estimate only: roughly 12-16 weeks for public launch plus the first
useful local portal release, assuming a designer, two developers, QA support
and an available Mission content/operations lead. External approvals, data
migration and government API access can extend this substantially.

Phase 1, weeks 1-2: discovery, content/URL inventory, service responsibilities,
approved scope and initial brand direction.
Phase 2, weeks 3-4: responsive design system, service templates, clickable
prototype and user/staff feedback.
Phase 3, weeks 5-8: public website, CMS, search, approved content, migration and
initial testing. Public launch is possible after its acceptance gates pass.
Phase 4, weeks 7-12: overlapping portal implementation for approved registration,
enquiries, local appointments, secure messages and staff queues.
Phase 5, weeks 13-16: end-to-end testing, accessibility/security review, training,
controlled migration, operational rehearsal and portal launch.
After launch: support period, field monitoring and evidence-led improvements.

For the competitive presentation, prepare a clearly labelled prototype in about
1-2 weeks if content/assets and design feedback are available. Show a polished
homepage, a complete service journey, mobile screens and a staff queue with demo
data. Prototype interactions must not be presented as completed live integrations.

13. ACCEPTANCE SCORECARD
========================
These are proposed project targets, not measurements of either existing site.

Findability: at least 90% of representative test participants find their assigned
priority service within 30 seconds; start with 8-12 participants across audiences.
Report this as a small usability study, not a population-wide statistical claim.

Responsive layout: agreed priority pages work at all specified widths, with no
unintended horizontal scroll and no fixed elements covering actions/errors.
Device coverage: current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari; physical Android and
iPhone checks for forms, menus, uploads and keyboard behaviour.

Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA assessment of representative templates and complete
critical processes; automated results plus manual keyboard/screen-reader evidence.

Content: every live service has approved requirements, correct destination,
contact/escalation route and review date. No placeholder identities or fees.

Performance: documented mobile lab budgets and post-launch field monitoring;
use the Core Web Vitals targets stated in work item W, not an invented score.

Portal integrity: cross-user access tests pass; required status changes are
auditable; retries do not duplicate requests; notification failures can be retried.

Appointments: no overbooking under the agreed concurrency test; holidays,
cancellations and rescheduling work in Nairobi local time.

Operations: successful backup restoration, monitored errors, rollback rehearsal,
account handover and staff training. Agree recovery time/data-loss objectives
with the Mission before selecting infrastructure and backup frequency.
